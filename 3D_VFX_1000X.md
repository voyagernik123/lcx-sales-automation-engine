### 3.3 · THE ASSET DECISION

E1, E6 and E8 want real modelled geometry (a room, an architectural volume, the machined mark).
Three routes:

1. **Procedural in code** — no asset pipeline, no bytes, limited to what maths can express.
2. **Blender → GLTF** — full control, needs a loader (~40 KB) and your Blender time. `blender-3d-track` memory says Blender MCP is an authoring tool, not a runtime; this is consistent with that.
3. **Blender → baked vertex data** → a `.bin` we load raw. No loader bytes, no flexibility, and a build step.

I recommend **1 for E1–E7** and **2 for E8 only** (the mark deserves real geometry and it is one
object). If you would rather model the theatre yourself in Blender, say so and I will build the
GLTF path instead — that changes the plan's shape, so it is a decision and not a detail.

## 6 · THE RULES THAT DO NOT BEND

These are what stop "cinematic" becoming "unusable", and each one is a scar.

1. **Every environment has a flat fallback that is not a downgrade in INFORMATION.** SSR, print,
   no-WebGL and reduced-motion all resolve to the existing surface. `useFlatChart` already
   starts `refused` for this reason.
2. **No idle animation.** `MOTION_POLICY` refuses a looping entrance by construction. A camera
   that drifts forever is an idle animation with a budget.
3. **Reduced motion resolves to the final frame, not a faster animation.** Already enforced.
4. **Text stays in the DOM.** Projected from the same matrix, never baked into a texture. It is
   the accessibility tree and the print path.
5. **Brand hex exact.** `assertBrandFidelity` runs on every new material. This is the constraint
   three.js will fight and the reason §3.1 recommends B.
6. **Absent data REFUSES.** A 3-D scene must never invent a surface through missing points.
   S9 stays blocked while its scores are `3 + ((i * 2) % 3)`.
7. **One shared GL context.** Sixty contexts will exhaust an 8 GB M1 — already a design
   constraint in `flat/shared.ts` and it now applies to environments too.
8. **Every claim gets a capture.** No exceptions, at the real aspect ratio.

### 11.2 · Why WebGPU is refused, in writing

The blueprint's Layer 4 is the only wholly-unbuilt architecture it proposes. **There are zero references to
WebGPU anywhere in this repo**, and the targets Layer 4 sets are already met by the WebGL2 path: P1 measured
**4.406 ms/frame, 227 fps at 3200×1480** for 10,000 instanced gaussian deposits plus a bright pass and four
separable blurs, on a real M1/8 GB via ANGLE Metal. Layer 4 asks to verify 60 fps for 10,000 points. That was
P0/P1's job and it returned 227.

So WebGPU would be a second rendering backend, maintained in parallel, to reach a number already reached —
and every one of §6's eight rules would have to hold on both. It is refused until a dataset appears that
WebGL2 genuinely cannot carry. The current particle path is also chosen for a reason WebGPU would cost:
`readState()` makes its claims assertable, and it has already caught two real bugs by counting live particles
against the expected total.

### 11.3 · What the audit found that the blueprint did not mention

Four defects in the lit shader, none of them in the blueprint, two of them found by the test written to prove
the first two. All four are fixed:

1. **`shadowTaps` was declared and never read.** The minimum tier paid nine texture fetches per lit fragment
   for a result it had asked to do without — on exactly the machines that cannot afford it.
2. **The anisotropic and isotropic GGX branches disagreed about what alpha means.** `at`/`ab` were derived
   from perceptual roughness and passed as alphas, so crossing `aniso > 0.001` jumped alpha from `rough²` to
   `rough`, widening the lobe. E8's mark uses that path.
3. **The anisotropic divide guard was clamping output.** `max(1e-8, v2)` sits above `v2`'s real floor of
   `(at·ab)² = 1.6e-11`, so smooth materials near the peak returned about two thirds of the correct intensity.
4. **The isotropic branch had the same defect, and it was live on the sign-in screen.** `max(1e-6, PI*d*d)`
   against a real floor of `PI·a2² = 5.3e-11` — five orders out. It fired for every material smoother than
   roughness 0.154 inside `NdotH > 0.9997`, the core of the highlight. Three shipped materials are in range
   and two are the LCX mark: `ForgeBackdrop` 0.13, `e8` 0.13, `e2` 0.14. At 0.13 the peak came back **3.9×
   too dim**; at the roughness clamp, 18,930×.

Defects 2 and 4 compounded on the same material: E8's mark had both a blurred and a clipped highlight, which
is most of the difference between machined metal and grey plastic.

**And one structural finding, larger than the one the plan named.** §10.1 records the quality ladder as the
decided answer, "wired into all nine". That is true of the nine harnesses and **false of the eight shipping
components** — every one of them hard-codes `createShadowMap(stage, 1024)` and full-quality AO and DOF, so a
weak machine gets the full frame with no degradation available. The ladder was never carried across the
promotion into the app. Worth stating precisely: the harnesses do not auto-select either — each reads the
tier from a URL parameter defaulting to `full`, and `pickQualityTier` (which requires a *measured* frame time
and refuses to guess from one) is called by nothing. So "wired into all nine" has always meant
*parameterisable*, not *adaptive*.

### 11.4 · What §7(b) can and cannot cover, now that it has been looked at properly

The plan said "measure §7(b) on all eight". Having built it out, that was wrong in two places, and both are
category errors rather than gaps:

- **E8 is NOT APPLICABLE.** Clause (b) asks whether an operator gets *their answer* faster. E8 carries no
  dataset and answers no question. It is gated on clause (a) alone. Recording it as "unmeasured" would imply
  outstanding work that does not exist.
- **E1 is DEFERRED with a reason.** Its panel text is injected from the other environments' READMEs at build
  time, so an answer key would rot on the next rebuild — and a stale key does not fail loudly, it marks
  correct answers wrong and reports a legible surface as illegible. E1 needs a question about its geometry
  rather than its copy, and it has no interactive addressing, so "which panel is addressed" has no answer
  either.

Six environments are therefore measurable, and six are covered: E2, E3, E4, E5, E6, E7.
