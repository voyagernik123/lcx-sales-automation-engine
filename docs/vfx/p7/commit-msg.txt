THE PRODUCTION (VFX_PRODUCTION_PLAN.md) P7, on P6 (8fc1206). The plan's line: "on arrival the rooms that changed light up in rank
order across the stage, the changed figures bloom once, the ticker turns over; then everything is perfectly still until the next
change. Nothing pulses, spins or breathes."

WHAT YOU WILL SEE
  On arrival (a fresh session, or a return after five minutes away) the ticker in the top bar reveals the watch's ranked items one
  per heartbeat — money, liability, deadline, activity — and, on the SAME heartbeat, the room each item belongs to lights on the
  stage behind the page, blooming once as it lights and settling on the next step. Rooms with change but no ranked item light when
  the sweep ends. Then nothing moves. The charts' arrival bloom (P5) and the figures' deltas (S6) are the same choreography's
  other surfaces.
  SEEN (docs/vfx/p7/stage-after-arrival-command-deck-dark.png — the bare stage, DOM hidden, after the sweep): bright pools under
  the rooms the watch ranked (sales, regulatory, distribution), the quiet glow P3 gives every held room under the other five, dark under
  none that is held. Before P7 (stage-before-p7-tasks-dark.png) every held room wore the SAME glow whatever had changed, because
  draw() never saw the watch at all (THE DEFECT below); the light said nothing.

THE MECHANISM — no new loop, no new clock
  · ONE sequence already existed: `useArrivalStore` steps `revealed` one item per heartbeat bucket (every(ARRIVAL_STEP_MS)) and the
    WatchStrip turns over on it. The stage now reads that counter: `roomLitAt(room, changed, watch, revealed, sweeping)` in
    stageScene.ts (pure; 4 tests: no watch → the P3 frame, rank order, the unranked tail, the bloom step) decides whether a room's
    glow is drawn and whether this is the step it lit on (ROOM_BLOOM ×1.35 size, ×1.25 intensity, clamped under the glow bound).
    Discrete by design: frames BETWEEN steps would be a scheduled animation, which the redraw ratchet refuses.

THE DEFECT THIS FIXES (P3): Stage.tsx's draw() read `watch` and `entitlements` from the closure captured at MOUNT (the setup
  effect has [] deps, rightly). The watch arrives by fetch after mount, so inside draw it was null for the life of the mount, and the
  redraw effect that fired on arrival redrew with the stale values. "Glows sized by the watch" had only ever held when the stage
  happened to mount after the watch had arrived. draw() now reads watch/entitlements/revealed/sweeping through refs written on
  every render — the mechanism the ratchet prescribes.

THE RATCHET EXTENDED — measured, not asserted
  · The instrument's clock is CONTROLLED, not merely frozen: FREEZE_ENV exposes `__lcxClockAdvance(ms)`; after the watch arrives the
    instrument advances the clock ARRIVAL_STEP_MS at a time and lets the real heartbeat deliver each step, until the strip reports
    rest (bounded at items + 2). Under the frozen clock the sweep sat at 0 for ever (smoke: items 3, steps 0, sweeping true) — the
    first version of this measurement would have read "no room ever lights" as the record.
  · Per capture: `arrival = { items, steps, sweeping, frames, clockSteps, framesDuringSweep }` from the strip's data-items /
    data-revealed / data-sweeping and a read-only `__LCX_STAGE_FRAMES` counter (draw() increments; disposed with the stage).
    Bounded means steps === items and framesDuringSweep ≤ items + 1. Smoke, /command-deck, both themes: items 3 · steps 3 ·
    clockSteps 3 · framesDuringSweep 3 · rAF at rest 0.
  · The objects wait also waits for the sweep to end before the rest window, so "at rest" is after the arrival, never during it.
