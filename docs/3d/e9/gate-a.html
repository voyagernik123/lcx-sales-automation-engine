§7(a) · the decision sheet

§7 gates every environment on (a) a stranger stops scrolling and (b) an operator still gets their answer at least as fast as the flat version, together. Clause (b) has an instrument. Clause (a) does not, and cannot — attention in the wild is not measurable at a desk by the people who built the thing, and a five-point scale would only be a number with no instrument behind it.

So this sheet does not measure (a). It makes the judgement cheap and even-handed: the same data drawn both ways, at the surface's real proportions, with the environment's own verdict beside it — and which panel is which stays hidden until you choose, because knowing which one cost three weeks is exactly the bias a decision sheet should not carry.

One handicap you must hold in mind, because it was found the hard way. Each flat panel is a single static capture, and several of these flat components take an azimuth an operator can rotate. A blind machine-reader trial on this same pair had three readers independently report that the flat surface's far face is simply undrawn at one azimuth — so a flat panel here can be missing data the shipping component would have shown a reader who turned it. Judge the arrest, which is what clause (a) asks; do not judge completeness from these panels.
